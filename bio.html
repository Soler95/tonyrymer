Biography

Boston-born cellist Tony Rymer has appeared as a concerto soloist with the Atlanta Symphony, the Boston Pops, the Cleveland Orchestra, the Detroit Symphony and the Pittsburgh Symphony, among others.

He won First Prize at the Sphinx Competition (Senior Division) and at the Washington International Competition, Second Prize at the George Enescu International Competition in 2014 and Third Prize at the Stulberg International String Competition. He has been heard as a soloist on WGBH Boston, WCLV Cleveland, NPR’s Performance Today and at the BBC Proms, and was one of the first recipients of the Jack Kent Cooke Young Artist Award on NPR’s From the Top.

Chamber music

Chamber music lies at the heart of his work. He has performed with Hilary Hahn, Itzhak Perlman, Midori, Ani Kavafian, Miriam Fried, Kim Kashkashian, Paul Katz, Martin Helmchen and Dénes Várjon, and with members of the Guarneri, Takács, Juilliard, Orion and Cleveland quartets.

He is a regular guest at festivals and concert series in the Americas, Europe and Asia, including Marlboro Music, the Ravinia Steans Music Institute, the Perlman Music Program, the Bard Music Festival, Krzyzowa Music, Festival del Lago, the Apple Hill Center for Chamber Music, the Leeds International Concert Series, Brooklyn Chamber Music Society and Musical Masterworks, and has appeared with the Kaleidoscope Chamber Collective.

Training

Tony Rymer began playing the cello at the age of five in Boston. He was a Project STEP scholarship student from 1996 to 2007 and attended the Walnut Hill School for the Arts. He earned his Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees at the New England Conservatory with Paul Katz and Laurence Lesser as holder of the Laurence Lesser Presidential Scholarship, and went on to complete a further Master of Music with highest marks in the class of Frans Helmerson at the Hochschule für Musik Hanns Eisler Berlin.

He has also worked privately with Yo-Yo Ma and taken part in master classes with Anner Bylsma, Gary Hoffman, Steven Isserlis and Pieter Wispelwey.

Instrument

He plays a cello by Jean-Baptiste Vuillaume, Paris, generously on loan from the Deutsche Stiftung Musikleben.
